## 1. Two security holes, and why they matter now

I went through the database rules line by line rather than assuming they were
fine. Two of them aren't.

**Anyone on the internet can download every photo in the app.** The photo storage
bucket was set to public read. That sounds like it only means "if someone has the
link, they can see the picture" — but it's worse than that. The same permission
also lets anyone *list* the bucket's contents. So a stranger can ask the database
"show me every folder," get back a list of every user, and walk it down to every
individual photo. The key needed to do this is inside the app itself, which is
normal and unavoidable — that key is designed to be public. It was just never
supposed to be the only lock on the door.

**The user table is also world-readable.** Anyone can pull the full list of users:
their internal ID, the name they typed at signup, and when they joined.

Either one alone is bad. Together they're worse, because they connect: pull the
user list to get a name and an ID, then use that ID to pull down that specific
named person's travel photos.

**Nobody has done this.** There's no evidence of misuse, and right now the only
data in there is yours. That's precisely why the timing is good — this is a
fifteen-minute problem today and a genuinely painful one the week after five
friends have uploaded photos of their families.

Both came from the same honest mistake: the rules were written permissively for
the public-blog feature we later postponed to V2. The permissions stayed; the
feature never arrived.

There's a third, milder issue — two of the AI functions check that a login token
was *sent* but never check that it's *real*. They're safe today because Supabase
blocks bad tokens before they reach our code. It's just a lock we're not holding
the key to ourselves.

**This is now first in my queue, ahead of everything else.** It lands before
anyone else's data exists.

### One thing to decide how you want to handle

You hold the master key to the database. You can read every note, every photo,
every blog your friends write. That's true of every app operator everywhere, and
it's also not what someone assumes when a friend hands them a *travel journal*.

I'd tell them directly, before they install — something like "I can technically
see your data, I'm not going to go looking, and you can delete all of it any
time." Thirty seconds of honesty now, versus a strange conversation later.

## 2. Where the app actually stands

Good shape. 322 tests passing, types clean, the whole V1 feature list is done and
merged. The iOS project is already regenerated with the right app name and ID.

Two loose threads:

**The forgot-password feature is built but not finished.** All the code is written
and committed. It's stuck on one thing: the password-reset email that Supabase
sends contains a *link*, but our screen asks for a *code* — and the template
doesn't include the code at all. So the email arrives and there's nothing to type
in. I've written the corrected email template into the plan file; it's a
copy-paste into the Supabase dashboard, then a five-minute test.

Worth finishing before your friends travel. Someone locked out of their account in
another country has no way back in.

**We decided to hide the Explore tab for version 1.0.** The community map shows
place names typed by other users, and Apple requires any app showing user-written
content to have reporting, filtering, and blocking — none of which we built,
because we deliberately deferred it. Rather than build moderation now, we hide the
tab. The data keeps quietly accumulating behind it, so nothing you built is
wasted, and it comes back in version 1.1 with moderation done properly. It's also
empty at launch anyway, until strangers start completing trips.

## 3. What I need from you

Roughly in order. The first one gates everything else, so it's worth doing before
you finish your coffee.

**1. Enroll in the Apple Developer Program — $99/year.**
Choose **Individual**, not Organization. Organization needs a company registration
number and can take weeks. Individual is usually approved in a day or two. One
tradeoff worth knowing: with an Individual account, **your legal name appears
publicly as the seller** on the App Store listing. If that bothers you, that's the
one reason to consider Organization.

**2. Three things in the Supabase dashboard.**
- Upgrade to the Pro plan ($25/month). Two reasons: the free tier **pauses itself
  after about a week of inactivity**, and if it's paused while Apple is reviewing
  the app, the reviewer opens a dead app and rejects it. Second, the free storage
  limit won't survive real users uploading photos.
- Fix the password-reset email template (the corrected version is in the plan
  file).
- Fix the Site URL if it's still pointing at `localhost`. New users get a
  "confirm your email" link that currently sends them to a dead page — their
  account does get confirmed, but they'll assume it failed.

**3. Write a privacy policy and a support page.**
Both are required fields; the listing can't be submitted without them. They can
live on rutvijdhotey.com. The privacy policy needs to say what you collect (email,
location, photos, voice) and that note text and photos are sent to Anthropic to
generate blogs. I can draft both if you'd like.

**4. App icon and splash screen.**
Still the default Expo placeholder. 1024×1024 PNG, and **no transparency** —
Apple rejects icons with an alpha channel. You convert the assets, I'll wire them
in.

**5. Screenshots and store copy** — later, once there's a build worth
photographing.

## 4. What I'll do

- The two security fixes, plus proper token checks on the AI functions.
- A Settings screen — currently there's nowhere to put anything. Sign-out moves
  here, along with the privacy links and the app version.
- **Account deletion.** Apple requires any app with signup to also offer deletion
  in-app. We don't have it, and it's an automatic rejection. It's more involved
  than it sounds — deleting an account has to clean up photos, notes, trips,
  blogs, and community contributions, and it can't be undone, so I want to design
  it carefully rather than bolt it on.
- Hide the Explore tab.
- Crash reporting, so that when a tester says "it crashed," we know where.
- A daily cap on blog generation, so one enthusiastic tester can't run up a bill.

## 6. The testing plan

**Five friends in the first wave. Ten to twelve total. Never all at once.**

Here's the reasoning, which shaped everything else:

**Your testers' trips can't be repeated.** Most beta tests let someone try again
tomorrow. This app only really gets exercised on an actual trip — real GPS drift,
real offline stretches, real four-hundred-photo days. If the build is broken while
your friend is in Lisbon, that trip is spent. You can't ask them to fly somewhere
again.

So: stagger by **departure date**, not by calendar. Pick people whose trips are at
least two weeks out, so a bug the first traveller hits gets fixed before the next
one leaves. And keep a few friends in reserve for the build *after* the next fix.

**You're tester number one**, on a real trip, before anyone else. Every bug you
find yourself is one you don't spend a friend's holiday discovering.

For the first wave, pick for variety: someone on a long multi-city trip (that's
what exercises the itinerary and location features), someone non-technical (they'll
hit the confusing first-run experience you can't see anymore), a heavy
photographer (that's where storage and cost show up), and ideally someone going
international.

Why not more than five: with one person fixing bugs in the evenings, five
travellers already generate more than you can keep up with — and it all arrives at
once, in the wrong timezone, impossible to reproduce at your desk.

A few things that make the difference between feedback and silence:
- Tell them **what to do**: capture notes as you go, generate the blog at the end.
  Otherwise half of them open it once at the airport and you learn nothing.
- Send a **known-issues list**, so five people don't all report the same thing.
- Set up a **group chat**. Most real feedback is a half-sentence nobody will file
  formally.
- After each trip, do a **20-minute call** where they walk through the app while
  you watch. Worth more than a month of messages.
- The question that actually matters: **did they show the blog to anyone?**
  Capturing notes is the feature. The blog is the point.
